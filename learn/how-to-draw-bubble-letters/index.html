How to Draw Bubble Letters

Pencil the plain letter as a thin skeleton, draw a rounded outline a finger-width around every stroke, then erase the skeleton and ink the outline. Each step is animated below.

Learn · Lettering ⏱ 3 min read

Key takeaways

  • A bubble letter is an outline drawn around the strokes of a plain letter, the same distance away all the way round.
  • Keeping that distance even is what separates a smooth bubble letter from a lumpy one.
  • Leave the holes open. Puff too far and the triangle in an A or the loop in a B closes up.
  • Trace a printed outline a few times, then draw it freehand.

Step 1: Pencil a thin skeleton

Write the letter lightly in pencil, one thin line per stroke. This is the skeleton the bubble goes around. You erase it later, so press lightly.

Plain capitals are the easiest place to start: straight lines and simple curves. Leave more space between letters than you normally would, because each one grows by a finger-width on every side.

Step 2: Puff an outline around every stroke

Draw a rounded outline about a finger-width away from the skeleton, all the way around each stroke. Go around the end of a line in a half-circle and around a dot in a full circle.

Where two strokes meet, like the middle of an H, their outlines join into one shape: draw only the outer edge. Keep the gap the same width everywhere. That one habit decides how the finished letters look.

Step 3: Round the corners, erase, ink and color

Smooth any sharp corners where two outlines meet, so the shape looks inflated rather than cut out. Erase the pencil skeleton.

Go over the outline in marker or pen, then color the inside. A dark outline around a lighter color is the easiest to read on a poster or a sign.

How puffy should a bubble letter be?

Aim for an outline about a finger-width from the skeleton at the size you are drawing. Closer than that and the letter stays a plain letter with a line around it. Much further and the holes start to close: the triangle inside an A, the loops of B, P and R, the gaps in an E.

If a hole closes, pull the outline in a little for that letter, or draw the whole word larger so the same puff fits.

Practice with a printed outline

Tracing is the quickest way to get the shape into your hand. Print a letter from our printable bubble letters, lay thin paper over it and trace it five or six times, then try it freehand. How to Draw Bubble Letters: FAQ

What is the easiest way to draw bubble letters?
Pencil the plain letter thinly, draw a rounded outline a finger-width around every stroke, then erase the pencil and ink the outline. Tracing a printed bubble letter a few times first makes the shape much easier to draw freehand.
Why do my bubble letters look lumpy?
The gap between the outline and the skeleton changes from place to place. Keep it the same width all the way around every stroke, and round off each corner where two outlines meet.
How do I make bubble letters overlap?
Draw the letters close enough that each outline crosses the one before it. Then erase the part of the later letter's outline that falls inside the earlier letter, so one looks tucked behind the other.
