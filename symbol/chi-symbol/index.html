Chi Symbol

The lowercase χ that powers the χ² statistical test, the capital Χ that opens "Christ" in Greek and gave us the Chi-Rho monogram and the "X" in Xmas — the one Greek letter forever mistaken for a Latin X. Click any symbol to copy it instantly.

Chi is the 22nd letter of the Greek alphabet, written χ in lowercase (U+03C7, GREEK SMALL LETTER CHI) and Χ in uppercase (U+03A7, GREEK CAPITAL LETTER CHI) — a glyph so close to the Latin X that the two are constantly confused. That resemblance is exactly why chi carries so much history. The capital Χ is the first letter of Χριστός (Christós), Greek for "Christ," and scribes have used it as shorthand for the name for over a thousand years — the spelling "Xres mæsse" appears in the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle around 1100, and the same habit is why English still writes "Xmas" today. Combine chi with the next letter, rho, and you get the Chi-Rho monogram ☧ (U+2627, CHI RHO), the early Christogram that Roman emperor Constantine I adopted as his military standard, the labarum, after a reported vision before the Battle of the Milvian Bridge on 28 October 312 CE — an account that reaches us through the ancient writers Eusebius and Lactantius rather than a neutral record. In modern math and science, lowercase χ is the workhorse of the chi-squared (χ²) test, the goodness-of-fit statistic Karl Pearson introduced in 1900 and now a staple of statistics and data science.
