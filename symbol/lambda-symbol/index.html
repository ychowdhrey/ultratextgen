Lambda Symbol

The lowercase λ that graphic designer Tom Doerr turned into a gay-liberation emblem in 1970, the same letter behind Alonzo Church's lambda calculus and physics' symbol for wavelength, and the capital Λ that stands for the cosmological constant. Click any symbol to copy it instantly.

Lambda's lowercase glyph, λ (U+03BB, GREEK SMALL LETTER LAMDA), is the 11th letter of the Greek alphabet — but it carries two of the heaviest meanings any single character has picked up. In 1970 the graphic designer Tom Doerr, a founding member of the Gay Activists Alliance (formed in New York in December 1969), chose the lowercase λ as the group's emblem — reportedly for its physics sense of 'a complete exchange of energy' — and by December 1974 the International Gay Rights Congress in Edinburgh had declared it the international symbol of gay and lesbian rights; it still names Lambda Legal and the Lambda Literary Foundation. The same letter anchors computing: Alonzo Church built his lambda calculus in the 1930s, the formal system that later gave Python its lambda keyword for anonymous functions (added in 1994) and Amazon its AWS Lambda serverless service. In physics λ is the standard symbol for wavelength, while the capital Λ (U+039B) denotes the cosmological constant — the dark-energy term in the ΛCDM model of the expanding universe. Both forms entered Unicode 1.1 in 1993.
