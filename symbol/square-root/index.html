History

Where the Square Root Symbol Comes From

The radical sign traces back to German algebra manuscripts of the 1400s, where mathematicians used a stylized dot or a shorthand letter "r" — short for the Latin radix, meaning root — to mark a root operation. The symbol close to the modern √ first appears in print in Christoff Rudolff's 1525 algebra textbook Coss, one of the earliest printed works on algebra in German.

The horizontal bar that extends over the number or expression being rooted (called the vinculum) was a later refinement, added to make clear exactly what the root applied to — useful once mathematicians started taking roots of entire expressions like √(a + b) rather than single numbers. By the 17th century the combined symbol had become the standard notation across European mathematics, and it hasn't changed meaningfully since.
