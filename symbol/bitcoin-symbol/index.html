History & Context

Where the Bitcoin symbol comes from

Most of the currency signs you can type were encoded by Unicode as a matter of routine text formatting — the character existed in the world, so it got a code point. The Bitcoin sign arrived differently. ₿ was added in Unicode version 10.0, released in 2017, notably recent next to the older signs it now sits beside in the Currency Symbols block. It was included specifically in response to real, organic demand from a fast-growing user base — not because a standards committee was tidying up currency formatting, but because a lot of people had started needing a symbol that didn't yet officially exist.

That origin shows up in how the symbol is used. Beyond writing a price or a tip line, ₿ functions as an identity marker — a tribal badge for "Bitcoin maximalists," people who believe Bitcoin specifically, not crypto in general, is the future of money, and who signal that belief by dropping the ₿ straight into a bio or display name. The style leans ostentatious and half-ironic-half-sincere, the kind of bio that jokes about "minimum brain, maximum Bitcoin" while entirely meaning it. In that role ₿ behaves less like ¢ or ₩ and more like a flag emoji or a political symbol: it's there to say something about the person, not to denominate a number.

Two things are worth keeping straight. ₿ is not the Thai baht sign ฿ (U+0E3F): the two share a B-with-a-vertical-stroke silhouette but are unrelated characters with different code points and different origins, and it's genuinely easy to mistype one for the other. And ₿ is not "BTC" — ₿ is the symbol, BTC is the three-letter ticker or currency code you see on exchanges, the same split that separates $ from USD.
