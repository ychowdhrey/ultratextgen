Distorted Face Emoji

The stretched, inflated face 🫪 — codepoint, meaning, alt-input shortcuts, and the documented design history behind Unicode 17.0's most talked-about new emoji. Click any symbol to copy it instantly.

🫪 DISTORTED FACE (U+1FAEA) is a face pulled out of shape — swollen, stretched, and slightly airborne. Unicode added it in version 17.0 on 9 September 2025, and within months it was the new emoji people actually used, winning both "Most Popular New Emoji" and "Most '2026' Emoji" at Emojipedia's World Emoji Awards. This page identifies the glyph itself — meaning, codepoint, and how to type it — then covers where the design genuinely came from, including the widely-repeated origin story that its own Unicode proposal does not support.

Meaning & Use

What the distorted face emoji means

Unicode doesn't assign emoji a single fixed meaning, but it does publish search keywords for each one through CLDR. For 🫪 those keywords are vulnerable, light, airy, and bloated — a cluster that points at exposure and weightlessness rather than any one emotion.

The emoji's own Unicode proposal is more specific. It documents three intended uses, and they map neatly onto how the glyph is actually used now:

  • Startled disbelief — the “Excuse me????” reaction, where a face visibly inflates in response to something absurd.
  • Inflated ego — a head that has, literally, gotten bigger.
  • Dread — the “oh no” face, distorted by anticipation of something about to go wrong.

In practice the first reading dominates. 🫪 has settled into the slot between 😳 and 😵‍💫: too overwhelmed for a plain flushed face, not yet fully scrambled.

History & Context

Where the design actually came from

🫪 was proposed as the “Inflatable Face Emoji” in document L2/24-250, submitted by emoji designer Jennifer Daniel on 8 July 2024. The proposal traces the visual idea across a deliberately wide set of references — Leonardo da Vinci's grotesque head studies, Mark Gruenwald's Squadron Supreme, Steven Universe, the sculptor Yang Maoyuan's inflated-animal works, Willy Wonka, and Who Framed Roger Rabbit. The distorted face is an old visual joke; the proposal's argument was that no emoji covered it.

The design also had a life before it was ever a character. Since 2020 it existed inside Google's Emoji Kitchen, where users mash two emoji together — and where the two-😳-Flushed-Faces combination became one of the most popular drawings on the whole feature. Google's shipped 🫪 artwork derives from that mashup, with one visible change: the raised eyebrows the Emoji Kitchen version had are gone.

One correction worth making, because it circulates widely: a number of consumer-tech outlets have compared 🫪 to the crushed-objects imagery in Apple's 2024 “Crush!” iPad advertisement. That comparison is a media observation, not a documented origin — neither the Unicode proposal nor Daniel's own retrospective essay on the emoji mentions the ad at all, and Emojipedia's own entry hedges it the same way. Treat the resemblance as coincidence unless a primary source says otherwise.

Availability

Support, and the awards it won

🫪 arrived with Unicode 17.0, published on 9 September 2025, in the Symbols and Pictographs Extended-A block. It was one of eight emoji in that release — alongside Fight Cloud, Hairy Creature, Orca, Landslide, Trombone, Treasure Chest and Ballet Dancer — and comfortably the one that broke out. It took both “Most Popular New Emoji” and “Most ‘2026’ Emoji” at the World Emoji Awards. Worth naming precisely: those awards are run by Emojipedia, explicitly for fun, and are not a Unicode Consortium program.

Because 17.0 is recent, support is still uneven — a device that hasn't taken the font update shows a blank box (tofu) instead of the face. Whether it renders depends on your operating system version, not on the app you're typing in. For the current platform-by-platform picture, see the Unicode 17.0 rollout tracker.

FAQ

Distorted face emoji frequently asked questions

What does the distorted face emoji mean?

Most often startled disbelief — the “excuse me????” reaction, where a face inflates in response to something absurd. Its Unicode proposal also documents two other intended readings: an inflated ego (a head that has literally gotten bigger) and dread. Unicode's own CLDR keywords for it are vulnerable, light, airy and bloated.

Is the distorted face emoji based on Apple's “Crush!” ad?

No — that link is a media comparison, not a documented origin. Several consumer-tech outlets noted a resemblance to Apple's 2024 “Crush!” iPad advertisement, but neither the emoji's Unicode proposal nor its designer's own retrospective mentions the ad. The design's real documented ancestor is a Google Emoji Kitchen mashup of two 😳 Flushed Face emoji that had existed since 2020.

When was the distorted face emoji added to Unicode?

It was added in Unicode 17.0, published on 9 September 2025, at codepoint U+1FAEA in the Symbols and Pictographs Extended-A block. It was proposed in July 2024 as the “Inflatable Face Emoji” in document L2/24-250.

Why does the distorted face emoji show as a blank box on my phone?

Because your device's emoji font predates Unicode 17.0. An empty rectangle — often called tofu — means the character arrived correctly but your system has no glyph to draw for it. It depends on your operating system version rather than the app you're typing in, so updating the OS is what fixes it. The person you sent it to may see it perfectly well even if you can't.

What's the difference between 🫪 and 🫠 melting face?

They point in opposite directions. 🫪 Distorted Face (U+1FAEA) inflates and stretches outward — startled, overwhelmed, or swollen with ego. 🫠 Melting Face (U+1FAE0, added in Unicode 14.0) collapses downward — embarrassment, dissolving under pressure, or a resigned “this is fine.” Both cover overwhelm; only one of them is expanding.
