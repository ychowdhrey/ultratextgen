History

Where the Yin Yang Symbol Comes From

The yin-yang concept originates in ancient Chinese cosmology and became central to Taoist philosophy: the idea that seemingly opposing forces — dark and light, passive and active, feminine and masculine — aren't really in conflict, but complementary halves of a single whole, each containing a "seed" of the other. That seed is represented by the small dot of the opposite color nested inside each half of the symbol.

The classic circular swirl design, formally called a taijitu, reached its familiar modern form during the Song Dynasty, roughly the 11th–12th century CE, though the underlying philosophical concept it illustrates is far older. It remains one of the most recognized symbols from Chinese philosophy anywhere in the world today.
