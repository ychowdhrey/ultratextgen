Zalgo Generator Benchmark 2026: Testing Creative Control Across 13 Tools

We ran 13 Zalgo and corrupted-text generators through one procedure on September 29, 2026: the same ten inputs, the same fifteen creative tasks, and 3,774 captured outputs measured on one scale.

Main finding No generator led every dimension. Two of the 12 we could test let you shape corruption across a sentence; four could remove Zalgo; the three fastest to a copied result (Glyphy, FontCraft, Pixelied) have no intensity, placement or shape controls.

Where UltraTextGen performed strongly

  • UltraTextGen was the only tested generator with a choice of mark family (all, bars, letters, symbols, noise) and a frequency control that sets how many letters get marks, separate from how many marks each gets.
  • Of the 10 creative tasks that need no shape control, UltraTextGen directly produced 9, Textavia's Glitch Text Generator 8, and Zalgo.io, Coddy and PSD-Dude 7 each. Counting all 15, it was 14 against 13 for Textavia; the task list includes several effects UltraTextGen names as presets, which favours it.
  • UltraTextGen's Mid position is the only amount-adjustable middle-only setting that measured 100% overlay marks. The other adjustable middle modes measured 0% to 90%, because their "middle" lists also hold marks that sit above or below. It mixes five overlay marks, so it does not draw one clean strike line.
  • UltraTextGen was the only tested generator that saves text and settings in the page URL and the only one that checks the output against platform limits (Discord message, Discord nickname, X post and Instagram bio) as you type.

Where other generators performed strongly

  • Textavia's Glitch Text Generator tracked its intended increase, decrease and peak profiles more closely than UltraTextGen's equivalents (r 0.94 to 0.95 against 0.81 to 0.86), and it offers 55 presets.
  • Zalgo.io was the only tested generator with a "same result each time" toggle and a platform-safe cap (it limits intensity to 5: 7.4 instead of 26.6 marks per letter on Extreme). It reaches a copied result in 2 interactions, with its input, output and Copy button in the first desktop screen.
  • PSD-Dude was the only tested generator with a separate amount for marks above, through and below, and it offers an animated GIF export.
  • Coddy gives the same output for the same text and settings, even after a reload, and combines above, through and below freely.
  • Glyphy, FontCraft and Pixelied need no settings at all: type once and copy. FontCraft's 400 styles include 12 fixed Zalgo levels that repeat exactly.
  • SymbolDB reached 105 marks on one letter, the highest measured classic ceiling that held. UltraTextGen measured 92 with every position on (100 with one position, by its code).

Shapes at 100% frequency

Version 1.0 could not test UltraTextGen's shapes at 100% frequency. Version 1.1 did. At 100%, every letter gets marks and only which marks is random, so each single output follows its shape as closely as the average: r 0.85 for Slope Up, Slope Down, Pyramid and Valley, and 0.91 for Steps. At the default 80% frequency, single outputs measured 0.37 to 0.52. Textavia's shape presets still fit better on average (0.94 to 0.95), while their single outputs measured about 0.60.

Decoders on other tools' output

Version 1.0 tested each decoder only on its own tool. Version 1.1 ran all 3,726 usable captured outputs through UltraTextGen's decoder and through the character ranges read in the other three decoders' page code. UltraTextGen's decoder left no combining mark in any output. The range Zalgo.io's code strips left marks in 3% of outputs, and the range LingoJam's and Zalgo.org's code strips left marks in 68%, because it stops at U+0361.

Decoding cannot restore letters a generator changed: Glyphy's underscores, PSD-Dude's added spaces and letter swaps, SymbolDB's symbol templates and FontCraft's dropped accents all survive every decoder.

What still favours other generators

  • Glyphy, FontCraft and Pixelied need no settings at all; UltraTextGen now matches their 2 interactions but shows far more controls.
  • Textavia's Glitch Text Generator offers 55 ready-made styles (result cards, not a menu) to UltraTextGen's 8 presets, and its averaged shape fit is closer.
  • Zalgo.io's "Consistent" toggle and PSD-Dude's per-zone sliders did the same jobs first; UltraTextGen now has equivalents, not unique features.
  • FontCraft's 400 styles include ready-made decorations no Zalgo engine produces.

Design

What the study asked and why

Research question. How much intentional control does each popular Zalgo generator give over the result, and what does its output measure like when every tool gets the same input?

Why. Zalgo generators look alike in search results and many pages claim "full control" or "50+ styles". We wanted to know which controls exist when you operate the page, not which ones the page copy lists. UltraTextGen builds one of these generators, so it is part of the sample and is tested the same way.

What counts as supported. A capability is supported only if the user can request it. Random output that happens to rise or peak does not count as a shape. A page claim with no working control is recorded as a claim, not a capability.

Averaged over 20 outputs, both tools implement what they promise. A single output is noisier on both, because each letter still gets a random number of marks: that is the result a user actually copies. Textavia's Edges Only is a hard edge on the first and last two letters rather than a graded valley, so it fits a valley poorly by design.

Waves. Both wave options repeat, and they are different waves. UltraTextGen's averaged profile has its dominant rhythm at 2 cycles across the text, where its other shapes peak at 1: two long swells, whatever the length. Textavia's repeats about every 6 letters (14 cycles), which shows more clearly in a single output.

Two cautions. The ideal valley and steps profiles match UltraTextGen's own formulas, so they favour its versions of those two shapes. And UltraTextGen ran at its default 80% frequency, which leaves about one letter in five unmarked and lowers its r; a run at 100% did not execute.

How they combine. UltraTextGen's shapes apply on top of any position, mark family, frequency and amount. Textavia's are fixed presets, each with its own marks and amount.

Analysis

How much intentional control each tool gives

We did not multiply slider positions into a number of "possible states". The controls are not independent (UltraTextGen's amount and shape both scale the same count), many settings look identical once rendered, and a big number would reward long sliders over useful ones. Instead we count seven independent axes a user can set on purpose. This model was built after the data was collected:

  1. amount of corruption,
  2. which zones get marks (above, middle, below),
  3. a separate amount per zone,
  4. which family of marks is used,
  5. how many letters are touched (frequency),
  6. how corruption changes along the text (shape),
  7. getting the same result again.
1 = a working control, 0.5 = partly (fixed styles, presets only, or a side effect), 0 = none. The total counts axes; it is not a quality score.

Record

Page claims that did not hold

Pages were read as well as operated. These claims did not match what the page did on September 29, 2026. UltraTextGen's own page is included. Its claims below were corrected on its English page in the same release as this study. On its translated pages, the statements about other generators and the Extreme tooltip were corrected too; the translated intro, decoder and sharing text still carry some older wording about UltraTextGen itself until their next update.

  • UltraTextGen said Extreme gives "up to 100 marks a letter"; with the default Position All it gives 92, and 100 only with a single position. It said the decoder "strips every Unicode combining mark" and that decoding is lossless; it strips six mark ranges, and like every tested decoder it removes accents typed as a separate mark. It said a shared link opens your "exact configuration"; the settings return, but the random marks change.
  • SymbolDB lists "50+ unique styles". Measured, the 50 templates reduce to about four mark patterns, and templates named Top, Middle or Bottom place marks about half above and half below.
  • PSD-Dude calls Glitch Intensity the master Zalgo control; in the page code it only sets how often letters are swapped for look-alikes, and it did not change the marks.
  • Zalgo.org's slider shows one height, but every keystroke resets it to 17.
  • Zalgo.io's text describes a separate slider for each direction; the page has three on/off direction buttons and one intensity slider. Its "Middle" button measured 15% middle marks.
  • Coddy's intensity slider starts at 1, but in its code values from 1 to 12 add no marks; at 1, all 10 captured outputs were plain text.

Platform claims on every page, including UltraTextGen's, were recorded but not tested, because no output was pasted into an app.

Method

Method

Environment. Chromium driven by Playwright 1.56, logged out, at 1280 × 800 and as an iPhone 13 profile (390 × 664 viewport). Each page was loaded live on September 29, 2026. Output was read from the page as text, never from a screenshot.

Planned before testing. The selection rule, the ten dimensions, the evidence types, the inputs, the fifteen tasks and the measurement definitions were written down before the first tool was tested. The method file was committed together with the first results, so that order rests on the session record. Eight amendments are recorded: Textavia counted as two tools, the phone viewport size, the shape-fidelity procedure, Creative Fabrica kept as untested, the text-integrity and extras rows, the control-axis model, the shape ideals and a record-keeping note. The text-integrity rows and the control-axis model were added after data collection.

Inputs. hello, HE COMES, something is watching me, 123456, café déjà vu, 👁 hello 👁, a 100-character sentence, a punctuation string, a string with runs of spaces, and a mixed-case string. 20 outputs of "hello" in each tool's default state (5 for FontCraft and Pixelied, which always repeat), 3 of every other input, and 5 to 10 per preset and per placement option.

Evidence types. Operated in a browser; read in the page's own code; measured from captured output. Page copy alone was recorded as a claim. When a cell needed a judgement, the rule was applied the same way to every tool, and each change a reviewer made is recorded in the data.

Measurement. Marks per letter counts combining marks on letters and digits. Zones use Unicode's canonical combining class: class 1 (overlay) is middle; classes 214, 216, 228, 230, 232 and 234 are above; 202, 218, 220, 222, 233 and 240 are below; any other mark, such as an enclosing mark or a Thai tone mark, counts as other. Length is UTF-16 code units, the count most text boxes and character counters report.

Review. After collection, an automated AI reviewer received the data with tool names replaced by letters and was asked for strengths by dimension, claims that should not be published, and a bias check. The method document still named UltraTextGen as the subject, so the review was not fully blind. It flagged that UltraTextGen led many dimensions and re-examined each lead against the data; the leads it did not uphold are not claimed here. Four more automated reviews then checked every number on this page against the data files.

Limitations

Limitations

  • The publisher is in the sample. UltraTextGen runs this study and one of the tools. The method written before testing, the published data and the automated review reduce that bias; they do not remove it.
  • The task list favours rich generators. Several tasks (tiny stack, roots, strikethrough) are effects UltraTextGen names as presets. Tasks were judged by their output, not by the name of a control, but a different task list could rank tools differently.
  • One day, one sample. Pages change. Every result is dated September 29, 2026. Search results were read from two indexes, logged out and in the US; Google's own results page could not be read by the test browser.
  • Creative Fabrica is untested. Any "only tested generator" statement here covers 12 generators, not 13.
  • Version 1.1 re-tested only UltraTextGen. UltraTextGen fixed its own gaps after reading this study, and only its test was re-run, on its updated build served locally the day before release. Competitors may also have changed since September 29.
  • Deeper evidence for UltraTextGen. UltraTextGen's code was read from its source and run in a test harness as well as operated in the browser; competitors' code was read from their live pages. That surfaced UltraTextGen's own false claims, but it is an asymmetry.
  • Embed was not searched for off-page. UltraTextGen has an embed page; we did not look for embed options on other sites beyond each generator page, so no "only" claim is made about embedding.
  • One emoji. The emoji test used 👁, a single character. UltraTextGen's code also adds marks inside multi-part emoji such as flags and family sequences, which splits them; that was read in the code, not measured here.
  • No app testing. Nothing here says whether output survives Discord, X, Instagram or Roblox.
  • Small task samples. Creative-task verdicts rest on 2 to 3 outputs each plus the page's code; shape fidelity rests on 20 outputs per shape.
  • A replicate, not a variant. A second batch of UltraTextGen shape outputs was meant to run at 100% frequency; the setting did not apply, so it is published as a replicate at the default.
