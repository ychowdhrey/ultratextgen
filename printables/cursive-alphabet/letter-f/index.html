How the cursive f works — joins, family, and the usual mistake

F belongs to the loop family (b, e, f, h, k, l) and is its only member that dives below the baseline. Curling the bottom loop to the left (like g) strands the exit stroke on the wrong side — f's bottom loop closes to the right.

Cursive F — FAQ

Undercurve up into a tall loop that touches the top line, like an l. Drive the spine straight down past the baseline into a bottom loop that curls back up to the right. Cross back through at the baseline and exit — f is the only lowercase letter with a loop above and below the line. Think of f as l on top and j underneath, stitched together at the baseline.
