How the cursive y works — joins, family, and the usual mistake

Y is an overcurve-family letter (m, n, v, x, y, z) with the g/j descender loop. A tail that stops at the baseline makes y read as u — commit to the dive below the line.

Cursive Y — FAQ

Overcurve into a rounded hump, like the first hump of n. Drop to the baseline, then keep going below it with a straight tail. Loop the tail to the left, swing back up, and cross at the baseline like g. y = n's hump + g's tail. If both parents are solid, y writes itself.
