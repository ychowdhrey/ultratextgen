How the cursive p works — joins, family, and the usual mistake

P is an undercurve-family letter (i, j, p, r, s, t, u, w) with a straight descender. Looping the descender makes p collapse into j — keep the downstroke straight.

The cursive capital P

The cursive capital P is a tall stem with one rounded lobe closing at the midline. In most school styles it stands alone and does not join to the next letter.

Cursive P — FAQ

Undercurve from the baseline to the midline. Drop a straight stem down past the baseline — no loop on the way down. Retrace up, draw a closed bowl that sits on the baseline, and exit right. p's stem is a plain stick, not a loop — that is what separates it from j.
