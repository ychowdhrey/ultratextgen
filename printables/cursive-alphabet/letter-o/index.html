How the cursive o works — joins, family, and the usual mistake

O is an oval-family letter (a, c, d, g, o, q) and the head of the top-joiners (o, b, v, w) — the four letters that hand over at the midline. Exiting from the baseline instead of the top breaks the bridge and makes ow read like au.

Cursive O — FAQ

Curve up and to the left, drawing a closed oval — the same start as a. Close the oval right where you began, at the top. Finish with a small sideways swing at the midline — o joins the next letter from the top, not the baseline. After o, the next letter starts at the midline — that is why ol looks different from al. Practice on, oo, and ol to feel it.
