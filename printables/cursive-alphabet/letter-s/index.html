How the cursive s works — joins, family, and the usual mistake

S is an undercurve-family letter (i, j, p, r, s, t, u, w); its closed-sail body is unique in the alphabet. Trying to snake a print-style S between the lines — the cursive letter is built from the undercurve, not from the print shape.

Cursive S — FAQ

Undercurve from the baseline up to the midline, like an i. Curve over and back down to the left, closing a small sail shape against your entry stroke. Touch the baseline and exit with an undercurve to the right. A correct cursive s does not look like a print s — it looks like a little sail or beak. If yours looks 'wrong', it is probably right.
