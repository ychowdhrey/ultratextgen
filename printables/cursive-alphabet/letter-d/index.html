How the cursive d works — joins, family, and the usual mistake

D is an oval-family letter (a, c, d, g, o, q): the oval comes first, the stem second. Looping the tall stem is the classic error — a looped d reads as dl. The stem is a plain retrace.

Cursive D — FAQ

Draw a closed oval exactly like a lowercase a. Retrace up the right side and keep going — d's stem rises to about three-quarter height, taller than the oval but with no loop. Slide back down the stem to the baseline and exit with an undercurve. Print the outline and trace the oval first, then add the stem — splitting the two motions stops the stem from leaning.
