How the cursive b works — joins, family, and the usual mistake

B is a loop-family letter (b, e, f, h, k, l) and a top-joiner: like o, v and w, it connects to the next letter from the midline, which changes that letter's entry stroke. Writers often drop b's exit swing to the baseline, which turns be into ba — keep the hand-off at the midline.

Cursive B — FAQ

Begin with an undercurve and swing up into a tall loop that touches the top line. Come straight down the loop's spine to the baseline. Curve up to the midline and finish with a small sideways swing to the right — b hands over to the next letter at the top. The tall loop should stay narrow — think of a blade of grass, not a balloon.
