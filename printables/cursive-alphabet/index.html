The four cursive letter families

Cursive is easiest taught in families that share an opening movement, not in A-to-Z order. The undercurve family (i, u, w, t, r, s, j, p) swings up from the baseline and is where beginners start. The overcurve family (m, n, v, x, y, z) enters over the top. The oval family (a, c, d, g, o, q) opens with the same curve-left oval. The loop family (l, b, e, f, h, k) rises into a tall loop. Master one member and its siblings come almost free — the full teaching order is in our guide, Cursive: when to start and which letters to teach first.

Cursive capital letters — the ones that surprise everyone

Most cursive capitals are recognizable, grander versions of print. Four are not: the capital G (a compound curve that looks nothing like print G), the capital Q (classically written like a large 2), the capital S (a swan-like double curve), and the capital I (an ornate compound stroke). Each letter page shows the capital form large, explains how it is written, and prints both cases on one sheet. Teach lowercase first — it is most of real writing — then bring capitals in through the child's own initials.

Learning cursive from the practice sheet

Print the practice sheet and trace each model letter until the joins feel natural, then move to the blank space to write it freehand. Study how each capital connects, and keep sessions short and frequent — a few letters a day beats one long sitting.
