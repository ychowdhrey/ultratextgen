Evil Eye & Hamsa Symbols

The nazar amulet 🧿, the hamsa hand 🪬, and the eye and charm symbols that ward off bad luck. Click any symbol to copy it instantly.

The evil eye 🧿 and the hamsa 🪬 are two different amulets from two different traditions, and this page keeps them apart. The nazar is a blue-and-white glass bead from the Turkish and Greek Mediterranean — the name comes from Arabic نظر (naẓar), meaning "sight" or "gazing" — and it guards against one specific thing: envy, the harmful look of a jealous eye. The hamsa is an open right hand from the Middle East and North Africa, named for Arabic خمسة (khamsah), "five," and it offers general protection rather than envy-specific protection. Many modern hamsa designs place an eye in the palm, which is why the two are so often treated as one charm — but they have separate origins and separate meanings. Frequently Asked Questions
What is the difference between the evil eye 🧿 and the hamsa 🪬?

They are two separate amulets. The nazar (🧿) is a blue glass bead from the Turkish and Greek Mediterranean that protects specifically against envy — the harmful gaze of a jealous person. The hamsa (🪬) is an open right hand from the Middle East and North Africa that offers general protection. They are often confused because many hamsa designs include an eye in the palm, but they come from different traditions and mean different things.

What does the nazar amulet emoji 🧿 mean?

🧿 is the Unicode Nazar Amulet, added in 2018. It depicts the blue-and-white glass bead — concentric rings of dark blue, light blue, white, and black — worn or hung to deflect the evil eye. The name comes from Arabic نظر (naẓar), meaning sight or gazing; in Turkish the bead is called nazar boncuğu. People use it in bios and captions as shorthand for protection and warding off bad energy.

Why does the hamsa emoji 🪬 show as an empty box?

The hamsa was added in Unicode 14.0 in 2021, which makes it one of the newer emoji. Devices and apps that have not updated their emoji fonts since then render it as a blank rectangle, often called tofu. The nazar amulet 🧿 is three years older and much more widely supported, so it is the safer choice when the symbol has to display everywhere.

What is the hamsa called in different traditions?

The name hamsa comes from Arabic خمسة (khamsah), meaning five, for the five fingers. In Islamic tradition it is the Hand of Fatima, after the daughter of the Prophet Muhammad. In Jewish tradition it is the Hand of Miriam, after the sister of Moses. In some Christian communities it is the Hand of Mary. The symbol predates all three and is often traced back to the Punic goddess Tanit of ancient Carthage.

Is the evil eye emoji good luck or bad luck?

The emoji is the protective charm, not the curse. The evil eye itself is the harmful look; the nazar bead is the object that absorbs and deflects it. So sending 🧿 reads as wishing protection on someone, not wishing harm. In Turkish and Greek custom a bead that cracks is said to have done its job by taking a look meant for its owner.
