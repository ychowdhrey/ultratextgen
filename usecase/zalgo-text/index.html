What is Glitch Text?

Glitch text is ordinary text layered with dozens of Unicode combining marks — special characters that attach visually to a base character rather than occupying their own space. A single combining mark is normal: it's how accented letters like é or ñ are encoded in Unicode. Glitch text adds many combining marks per character, causing them to stack chaotically above and below the baseline and collide with adjacent lines.

The result looks glitchy, cursed, or corrupted — like the text itself is falling apart. It's widely used in horror-themed posts, creepypasta, meme captions, Halloween content, and anywhere you want a "corrupted data" aesthetic.

Internet culture actually calls this effect Zalgo text, after a 2004 running gag by comics artist Dave Kelly, where wholesome newspaper strips like Nancy and Archie were "corrupted" by an eldritch entity called Zalgo — announced with the phrase "H̸e̴ ̷c̶o̵m̸e̶s̷". The meme took off around 2009 as "the Nezperdian hive-mind of chaos," and the stacked-diacritics look has carried the name ever since — "glitch text" and "Zalgo text" describe the exact same effect, so this page (and the FAQ below) uses both interchangeably.

Tip for horror writers: set Shape to Slope Up and the corruption builds gradually across your sentence — clean at the start, unreadable by the end. It's the classic creepypasta "something is taking over this document" effect, and no slider-only generator can do it.

How This Generator Works

Characters controls which type of combining marks are used — bars (strikethrough-style), letters (superscript letter marks), symbols (enclosing/overlay marks), or noise (accent-style diacritics). "All" uses the full Unicode pool.

Position controls where marks attach — above, below, through the middle, or a combination. "Up & Down" gives the classic zalgo look with spires and roots but no strikethrough.

Shape controls how mark density varies across your text. Uniform applies even density everywhere. Slope Up starts clean and builds chaos. Wave oscillates. Pyramid peaks in the middle. Each shape creates a different visual rhythm.

Frequency is the probability that any given character receives marks at all. At 50%, roughly half your characters stay clean — great for a "corruption spreading" look. At 100%, every character is affected.

Amplitude is how many marks stack on each affected character. At 1, it's a subtle accent. At 20, it's maximum visual chaos.

Combining marks vs. "double stack" text: The same Unicode marks behind zalgo also power the "double stack" or Leͥgeͣnͫd look, where a small letter sits on top of each character — that's simply zalgo with Characters set to Letters, Position Up, and Amplitude 1. Where to Use Zalgo Text (and Where It Breaks)

Discord is zalgo's home turf — messages render combining marks fully, and no filter blocks them by default (server AutoMod or the MEE6 bot can). Display names and nicknames accept zalgo but cap at 32 characters, and every mark counts — use the Subtle preset for names. Discord usernames (post-2023) are lowercase letters, digits, underscores and periods only, so zalgo is impossible there.

Roblox filters aggressively: heavy zalgo becomes # symbols or boxes, and middle (overlay) marks are the first to break. If you want to try, use the Subtle preset, set Position to Up & Down, keep it short, and test in-game — full breakdown in does zalgo work on Roblox.

Instagram supports zalgo in bios, captions and comments — lighter styles work best, and very tall stacks get clipped by line-height. Usernames reject it. TikTok behaves the same: bios, captions and comments yes, usernames no.

Twitter / X renders zalgo in posts, bios and replies, but every combining mark counts toward the 280-character limit — heavy zalgo on a single word can eat half a tweet. Watch the "Will it fit?" badges under the output, or run any text through the character counter to check it against 25 platform limits at once.

Twitch usernames are plain alphanumeric (no zalgo), but chat renders it — though channel bots often strip or time out heavy glitch text. Minecraft chat, signs, and server MOTDs render combining marks with no filter. Steam renders zalgo unclipped, which is exactly why it overflows profile and group name UI — use sparingly. WhatsApp and Telegram messages render it fine.

A note on etiquette: screen readers announce every combining mark, turning one zalgo word into a long unpleasant ramble, and some communities (fan wikis, forums) ban zalgo outright for breaking layouts. Horror writers' rule of thumb: one zalgo line per ~200 words hits harder than a full corrupted paragraph. And if anyone worries the cursed text could harm their device — it can't: zalgo is Unicode, not code.
