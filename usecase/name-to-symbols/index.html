Which Script Should You Pick?

They are not interchangeable — they trade legibility against distinctiveness, and the right pick depends on whether people need to read the name or just recognise it. The generator above gives you every rare-script version at once, so the real choice is which one to copy.

  • Runes and Old Italic — the strongest “symbol” look, and the least readable. Best for a display name or aesthetic post where the shape matters more than the word.
  • Cherokee and Canadian Syllabics — rounded and distinctive, with good coverage on modern devices. A middle ground.
  • Tibetan, Ethiopic, Yi, Bopomofo — the most ornate. Also the most likely to show as boxes (□) on an older phone that has no font for them.
  • Faux Cyrillic and faux Greek — not in the grid above (they live on the faux fonts page), but worth knowing about: they keep your name obviously readable while still looking off-kilter, and they tend to pass username filters when the rarer scripts get rejected.

One quirk worth knowing: these alphabets were never designed to line up with the Latin 26, so some letters share a shape. In Elder Futhark, C, K and Q all become ᚲ. That is the script, not a glitch — and it is part of why the same name looks different in each style.

Where Symbol Names Work — and Where They Don’t

Because the output is ordinary Unicode text, it generally pastes cleanly into bios, captions, posts, comments and display names on TikTok, Instagram, Discord, X, Facebook and WhatsApp.

Two places it reliably breaks. First, @handles and login usernames: most platforms restrict those to Latin letters, digits, dots and underscores, so a rune name will be rejected there even though it works fine as your display name. Second, in-game names in many titles, which enforce their own character allowlists. Check with the platform checker above before you spend a rename token.

Also worth remembering: rendering depends on the reader’s device, not yours. If their phone has no font covering Tibetan, they see boxes. The closer a script is to Latin, the safer it is.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a name to symbols converter actually do?

It rewrites each letter of your name as a visually similar character from a different Unicode script — so "Maya" becomes ᛗᚨᛦᚨ in Elder Futhark runes, or 𐌌𐌀𐌙𐌀 in Old Italic. Nothing is an image and nothing is a font file: every result is real text made of standard Unicode characters, which is why it survives a copy and paste into a profile field.

Will these symbol names work on TikTok, Instagram and Discord?

Usually yes for bios, posts and display names, because they are ordinary Unicode characters. Some platforms restrict what may appear in a username or handle specifically, and a few will reject non-Latin scripts there even when they allow them in a bio. Paste it into the field and check before saving — and note that older Android and Windows builds may show a box (□) for the rarer scripts if the device has no font covering them.

Why do some letters look the same or repeat?

Ancient scripts were not built to match the 26-letter Latin alphabet, so a few letters have no close counterpart and reuse the nearest shape. In Elder Futhark, for example, C, K and Q all map to ᚲ. That is a property of the script, not a bug — it is also why the same name can look slightly different across styles here.

Is it free, and do I need an account?

Yes, free, and no account. Everything runs in your browser — your name is never uploaded or stored anywhere.

Can I use symbol letters in a gamertag or in-game name?

It depends on the game. Many games restrict names to Latin characters and will reject rare scripts outright; others accept them. Check the rules for your game before spending a rename, and if a name is rejected, try a style closer to the Latin alphabet — small caps or faux Cyrillic usually pass where runes do not.
