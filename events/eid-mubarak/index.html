About

Eid Mubarak marks two of the biggest greeting-and-gifting occasions on the Islamic lunar calendar: Eid al-Fitr, celebrated at the end of Ramadan, and Eid al-Adha, marked during the Hajj season a couple of months later. Both are celebrated with prayer, family gatherings, sweets, and messages wishing loved ones a blessed holiday. This page gathers everything for styling that greeting: curated fonts you can preview on your own text, the crescent moon and mosque emoji people actually paste, kaomoji for a joyful chat message, a few original ASCII art pieces, and a phrase bank of real greetings (native script, romanization, and translation) that style instantly when you tap them.

Eid Mubarak: twice a year, set by the lunar Islamic (Hijri) calendar: Eid al-Fitr after Ramadan, and Eid al-Adha around two months later

Eid Mubarak moves each year against the Gregorian calendar because it follows the Hijri (Islamic lunar) calendar, so it lands about 11 days earlier than the year before. The date shown at the top of this page is calculated for the current year; because the start of the month is confirmed locally by moon sighting, some countries observe it a day either side.
